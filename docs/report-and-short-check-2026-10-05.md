# Short home check and report update

- Supported everyday areas ask 4–5 core questions (full bathroom and kitchen 5, half bath 4). Clinical templates and historical report versions remain unchanged.
- Earlier answered details remain in family reports, guidance and consented handoff snapshots. Report percentages explicitly use the questions included in that report; concerns and uncertainty use answered questions as the denominator. Empty answers do not create a risk rating.
- The qualitative reported-concern scale describes follow-up priority, not a validated clinical instrument, fall probability, diagnosis or a declaration of safety. A previously authored weight-3 reported concern or reported movement/fall difficulty highlights professional review. Unknowns stay distinct from reported concerns.
- One next-steps section combines priorities, practical modifications, daily-life support, routines and optional technology. Requests route to the existing OT assessment or care-navigation service; the latter is labeled as a MyIntel aging-in-place follow-up with Austin. No new scheduling system, availability promise or automatic sharing is introduced.
- General home-safety themes were checked against CDC resources: https://www.cdc.gov/falls/prevention/index.html and https://www.cdc.gov/steadi/patient-resources/index.html. This shortened question selection and review-priority scale are MyIntel product choices, not CDC-endorsed or clinically validated.
- Human requirements remain: OT review of the revised content, supervised senior usability/timing tests, and confirmation of actual professional availability. No 10–15-minute completion claim has been verified.
