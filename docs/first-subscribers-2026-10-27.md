# HomeCheck: first Colorado subscribers

Prepared October 27, 2026. This is a proposed, founder-led acquisition plan and reusable draft material. No messages, CRM changes, paid advertisements, association placements or public claims were published.

## Start with one narrow buyer

Prioritize Colorado OT practices with one to five assessors that already perform home visits or aging-in-place consultations. Their immediate problem is turning a moving, conversational home visit into a clear report and follow-up plan without losing observations. The buyer is usually the practice owner or the clinician who currently builds reports manually.

Independent aging-in-place specialists are a secondary discovery audience and referral channel. Ask which parts of the workflow fit their training and services. Do not promise clinical access, OT credentials or AMPS administration from a subscription. MyIntel's confirmed direct services are fall detection, smart door locks and medication reminders; other modifications and OT care are referral/coordination.

Positioning: **Turn a home visit into a clear, prioritized action plan, with observations, area notes and follow-up in one place.**

Show the client-first report, the walkthrough and before-leaving review, preserved signed versions and intentional team summaries. Keep resident answers distinct from professional judgement. Say what has been built and demonstrated. Do not claim clinical validation, insurance reimbursement, guaranteed time savings, an independently verified safety percentage, AMPS scoring or an available provider network.

## Offer and pricing boundaries

The owner-approved individual-professional model on the separate billing branch is one free demo, then $19 per assessment or $49/month including five monthly assessment credits, with explicitly purchased $10 additional credits for subscribers. Monthly credits do not roll over. These are software fees, not the price of an OT's visit or MyIntel's installation service.

That billing branch is not merged into this report/organization review build. Public payment collection remains disabled. Confirm the final released checkout, credit accounting and cancellation terms before presenting the offer as purchasable. The new organization workspace has no team price, shared credit pool or automatic seat charge. Do not describe one $49 subscription as covering the whole practice.

Use a fictional demo first. Real client use requires the actual privacy/contract arrangements, appropriate consent and professional approval. Do not invent a discount or give away additional credits without a separate business decision.

## First 30 days after demo/release readiness

The following numbers are proposed targets, not a forecast or existing pipeline: **10 qualified conversations, 5 guided demos/trials, 3 paying individual professionals**, followed by evidence that they use it for a second assessment. A retained user is more useful than a large list of free signups.

Austin owns this small campaign. His stated operations availability is three days a week; agree on onboarding times individually rather than promising daily or immediate support. Record only business contact and product-usage milestones in the acquisition tracker, not client names or health answers.

## Channels in order

1. **Existing OT pilot and warm professional introductions.** Use a real conversation about their workflow, then a fictional sample. Ask for an introduction only after the OT has actually tried it and agrees. The senior demo-home participant is a separate usability opportunity, not an authorized testimonial or public case study.
2. **Colorado OT community.** OTAC has professional education, networking and sponsorship channels. Ask about a future member demonstration, newsletter or educational opportunity and its terms. The listed 2026 conference was September 24-27 and is already past; do not budget or sell access to that event now. No placement, partnership or endorsement has been arranged. [OTAC](https://otacco.org/), [official sponsorship page](https://otacco.org/Sponsor-and-Exhibitor-Registration).
3. **Useful founder content.** Prepare a short screen recording with fictional data: moving through an entrance/bathroom visit, capturing an unresolved observation, reviewing findings and producing a prioritized client plan. Publish only after review. Pair it with an article such as “What gets missed between a home visit and the finished report?” Invite a workflow conversation rather than promising clinical outcomes.
4. **Aging-in-place referral relationships.** Later approach independently verified Colorado home-modification and senior-service contacts whose workflow fits. Clearly explain direct technology services versus referral coordination. Check existing relationship ownership first. This plan does not identify any organization as a confirmed partner or available provider.
5. **National paid channels later.** AOTA offers advertiser and sponsorship channels. Consider a small placement only after the first users complete reports, some convert and some return. Confirm current inventory and cost directly before any spend. [AOTA advertiser information](https://www.aota.org/about/for-advertisers), [media planner](https://www.aota.org/about/for-advertisers/media-planner/).

Avoid buying a large list or running broad paid ads at the start. First learn which professional finishes a useful report and returns. The family home check can generate MyIntel service inquiries, but family inquiries and professional software subscribers are different acquisition funnels.

## A useful 20-minute demo

| Minutes | Show or ask |
| --- | --- |
| 0-4 | Ask how often they assess homes, who pays for report-writing time, which instruments they use and how they handle follow-up today. |
| 4-10 | Use a clearly fictional case. Confirm layout, capture a client goal and area notes, mark an observed concern and an unresolved item. Show resident perspective separately. |
| 10-14 | Show the client summary, category findings percentages, prioritized actions and detailed findings. Explain the percentage denominator and its limitations. |
| 14-17 | Show preserved signed versions and a separately consented team summary. Demonstrate what another member cannot see. |
| 17-20 | Ask what they would still edit, what would prevent real use and whether they would pay the stated individual price once released. Agree on one next step. |

Before a real pilot, ask about their training/scope, client authorization, record storage/retention requirements, device/network conditions and desired export. Do not administer proprietary tests or assume publisher permission. HomeCheck records authorized external results; it does not administer or score AMPS.

## Track learning, then conversion

Use a small manual, reviewed tracker initially: practice, contact, public source, existing CRM owner/history, outreach approval, conversation date, demo booked/completed, first demo report completed, main objection, paid plan when available, second assessment, next agreed follow-up and testimonial permission. Keep referrals, family service leads and professional subscription leads distinct.

Measure qualified conversation to demo, demo to usable report, usable report to paid, and paid to second assessment. Record actual time observations with participant permission before making efficiency claims. The existing family lead funnel is not a professional SaaS activation dashboard, and no new marketing analytics or CRM automation was added by this change.

The next spending decision should follow evidence from these first users. No ad budget, association fee, team price, revenue forecast or customer outcome is approved by this plan.
